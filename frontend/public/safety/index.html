SIMP Safety Center

Last updated: 2026-08-21

If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number first. United States: 911 · United Kingdom: 999 · EU: 112 · Australia: 000 · Canada: 911. After you are safe, use the in-app Report button — Trust & Safety is on call 24/7.

1. SIMP's safety commitments

  • Human moderators review every report within 24 hours. Severe-safety reports (imminent harm, sexual assault, minors) are escalated immediately.
  • Zero tolerance for payment-for-intimacy: any user who solicits money, gifts, or "verification payments" is permanently banned.
  • Zero tolerance for minors: any account we can determine is under 18 is removed within 24 hours.
  • Photo verification is available to all members and required for SIMP Elite — a selfie is compared to your profile photos, and the verification selfie is permanently deleted after review.
  • Block in one tap. Blocked users can no longer see your profile, message you, or appear in your discovery.
  • Privacy by default. Your exact location is never shown to other users — only an approximate distance.
  • We never ask for your password, payment card, or one-time code. Anyone doing so is a scammer; report and block.

2. Common scams to watch for

2.1 Money-for-intimacy ("sugaring" / "allowance" scams)

Anyone who asks you for money, gift cards, crypto, "verification fees," "travel reimbursement," or signals that intimacy is conditional on a payment is committing fraud and will be banned. SIMP's terms forbid this regardless of how it's framed.

What to do: stop replying · block · report with reason "Asks for money"

2.2 Investment / crypto "opportunities"

A match moves the conversation off the app quickly, builds trust, then pitches a trading platform, "mentorship," or crypto project. These are scams. Real investors do not recruit strangers on dating apps.

What to do: stop replying · block · report with reason "Off-platform scam"

2.3 Phishing links

A match sends you a link to "verify your profile," "see their photos," or "join their OnlyFans / Fansly / private site." These sites harvest your card or your login credentials.

What to do: do not click · block · report with reason "Phishing link"

2.4 Intimate-image extortion ("sextortion")

A match pressures you for intimate images, then threatens to publish them unless you pay. This is a crime in most jurisdictions. Paying does not stop them — it usually escalates.

What to do:

  1. Do not pay.
  2. Stop responding immediately.
  3. Document everything (screenshots, with timestamps).
  4. Report on SIMP with reason "Sextortion" — we will preserve evidence and cooperate with law enforcement.
  5. Report to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (US) or your local cybercrime unit.

2.5 Romance Bait-and-Switch

A match builds intense emotional rapport for days or weeks, then asks for a loan, a crypto transfer, or to deposit a check on their behalf. These are confidence frauds.

What to do: never send money to someone you have not met in person · never deposit checks from strangers · block · report

2.6 Account-takeover via "support" impersonation

Someone claims to be SIMP support (we will never DM you first), asks for your password, a one-time code, or payment details. We never ask for these.

What to do: block · report · if you already shared credentials, change your password immediately and contact safety@sim-p.app

3. Meeting in person for the first time

When you're ready to meet a match offline:

  1. Meet in a public place — a busy café, restaurant, or park.
  2. Tell a friend where you're going, who you're meeting, and when you expect to be back.
  3. Use your own transportation so you can leave on your own schedule.
  4. Keep your phone charged and on you.
  5. Don't share your home address until you've met several times and built trust.
  6. Limit alcohol and never leave a drink unattended.
  7. Trust your gut. If something feels off, leave. You owe no explanation.

4. Consent & boundaries

  • Consent is ongoing. "Yes" to one activity does not mean "yes" to another.
  • Consent can be withdrawn at any time. If your partner says stop, you stop.
  • If something happened that you did not consent to, you can report it to SIMP regardless of when it happened. We retain report evidence.
  • Coercion, intoxication-based, or unconscious-person sexual activity is sexual assault. SIMP cooperates fully with law enforcement on these reports.

5. Reporting & blocking

  • Open the user's profile → ⋯ → Report or Block.
  • Choose the most specific reason. The more detail you provide, the faster we can act.
  • Reports are visible to the reported user only as "Your account was reviewed"; they do not see your identity, your reason, or any other details.
  • If you don't want to report in-app, email safety@sim-p.app with as much detail as you can — screenshots, dates, the other person's profile URL.
  • If you report a user for an issue that requires law enforcement (sexual assault, threats, extortion), we will preserve the relevant evidence for at least one year even if you delete your account, so it remains available if you choose to file a report.

7. Privacy & data minimization

  • Only your age, display name, and optional city are visible by default.
  • Approximate distance is shown, not your exact location.
  • You can hide your age, hide your distance, or go invisible at any time in Settings → Discovery.
  • Block a user to prevent them from seeing your profile or messaging you.
  • Report a user to escalate to Trust & Safety.
  • See the Privacy Policy for full data-handling details.

8. Trust & Safety — what we do with a report

  1. Receipt — reports are visible immediately in the moderation queue.
  2. Triage — severe-safety reports are flagged for immediate action; routine reports go to standard review.
  3. Review — a human moderator evaluates the report, the user's history, and any related reports.
  4. Action — outcomes can include: warning, temporary suspension, permanent ban, account deletion, evidence preservation for law enforcement.
  5. Follow-up — the reporter is notified of the outcome (without revealing actions taken against the other user).
